Client workspace in Notion

How I organize a client implementation in Notion so engineers, analysts and the client all work from one place: a configuration change log that tracks every request from ask to verified, and a sourced knowledge base that keeps what we learn findable.

My role: Maintained the Notion client pages for the team's engineers and analysts, one of 23 I kept up to date.

1. CRM configuration change log

Every change to the client's CRM gets one row with a phase, area, owner, status and target date. Each row also records how we'll verify it and why it matters, so nothing is marked done until someone has tested it as a user. Click any row to open it.

2. Sourced knowledge base

Facts we learn during an engagement go into a knowledge base with a category, workstream, status and the exact source document. That way the next person starts from the primary source instead of re-asking the client or re-reading meeting transcripts.

Fact. The current install-to-invoice process inside the field-service system is fully documented, step by step, in the process flow document from the onsite interviews.

Installation (steps 32–33): the install team completes the job, marks items complete or incomplete with photos, and marks the work order installed. If payment is collected on site, the field app creates an invoice on the work order and takes card or ACH payment, or emails the invoice for online payment.

Invoicing (steps 34–36): accounting monitors work orders in "Ready to Invoice" and draft invoices, verifies and sends them, and works overdue invoices by phone. Cash and check payments are entered as receipts and applied to invoices.

Accounting side: not documented. The process map shows only that the field-service system syncs to QuickBooks daily, and that duplicate customers make the sync fail. An audit of the client's Zapier automations confirmed none of them touch QuickBooks, so the sync is a built-in feature, not Zapier glue.

💡Why it matters. If anyone needs to document or audit invoicing again, start with sections 9–10 of the process flow document. The QuickBooks-side procedure remains an open documentation gap.

Why it works

  • One source of truth. The client, engineers and analysts see the same list, so status meetings start from facts instead of memory.
  • Done means verified. Every change carries its own test, and the client re-checks it as a user before it closes.
  • Views for every audience. The same data shows as a full table, a status board, open issues or completed work.
  • Knowledge that lasts. Sourced entries mean new team members ramp up fast and nobody re-asks the client the same question.
