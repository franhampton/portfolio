Change impact matrix: current state vs. future state

For a multi-business-unit ERP move from QuickBooks to NetSuite, I compared each current-state process map with its future-state design and recorded what changes: systems, steps, roles and controls, plus the open questions the process team still needed to answer.

How to read it

Each process compares the current-state map with the future-state design: how many systems each uses, what kind of change it is, and who it affects. Open items are questions the process team still has to answer, recorded instead of guessed.

Why it's useful

  • Leaders see the size of the change at a glance: fewer systems, which roles shift, and where steps are added or removed.
  • Training and change management know where to focus, because every role and process impact is called out.
  • Gaps stay visible. Missing current-state maps and unanswered questions are logged as open items for the process team instead of being filled in by assumption.

Client, business unit and parent-company names are removed, and bank and map references are generalized. Software names are unchanged.
