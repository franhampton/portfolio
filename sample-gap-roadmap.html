Gap analysis and roadmap · sample

From interview findings to a costed 12-month roadmap

How I turn what stakeholders tell me into a business case: find the gaps, show what they cost, then sequence the fixes into waves leadership can approve. The figures come from my assessment work: $230K in phantom inventory and quadruple data entry, and a $999K-a-year, three-wave roadmap for an $80M B2B organization.

My role: Identified the gaps through stakeholder interviews and process mapping; my findings fed the 12-month, three-wave roadmap and the opportunity sizing.

How the analysis works

1 · Listen

Interview people in every role, from the counter and warehouse to finance and leadership, and record who said what.

2 · Map

Build current-state process maps and validate them with the people who do the work.

3 · Find the gaps

Flag duplicate entry, manual workarounds, disconnected systems and data that can't be trusted.

4 · Size and sequence

Estimate what each gap costs, then order the fixes by value, effort and dependency.

The 12-month, three-wave roadmap

Each wave delivers value on its own and sets up the next, so the client can pause between waves without losing what they've gained.

Wave 1 · Months 1–3

Stop the leaks

  • Fix inventory counts and receiving so the books match the shelf
  • Remove the most painful double entry with quick automations
  • Standardize the handful of reports leadership uses every week
Wave 2 · Months 4–8

Connect the systems

  • Integrate point of sale, billing and accounting so data is entered once
  • Retire spreadsheets that duplicate system data
  • Document and cross-train the key-person steps
Wave 3 · Months 9–12

Run on live data

  • Live dashboards instead of hand-built reports
  • Automated exception alerts for inventory and billing
  • A governance rhythm to keep processes and data clean

Sized opportunity: $999K per year, across reduced manual effort, recovered revenue and lower risk, presented with the assumptions behind each estimate so leadership could test them.

Why this approach lands with leadership

  • Every finding traces to a person. Each gap ties back to what someone in that role actually said, so nothing reads as a consultant's opinion.
  • Dollars, not adjectives. Sizing each gap turns "this is painful" into a number that can be weighed against cost.
  • Sequenced for momentum. Early wins in Wave 1 build the trust and budget for the bigger integration work.

Client name and identifying details are removed. The headline figures match my real engagements; the gap descriptions, severities and wave contents are generalized for the sample.
